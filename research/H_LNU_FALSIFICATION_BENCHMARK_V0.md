# H-LNU Falsification Benchmark V0

Status: **research benchmark design; no universal-law claim**.

## 1. Question under test

The benchmark tests the hypothesis:

> A provider-neutral core based on possibilities, hard restrictions, governed equivalence/quotient structure and invariants can discover, compare and realize useful laws across radically different held-out domains without domain-specific knowledge in the acquisition core.

The benchmark is designed to **falsify or delimit** this hypothesis, not to confirm it by construction.

## 2. What does not count as success

The following are explicitly insufficient:

- fitting held-out outputs without recovering any transferable structure;
- memorizing benchmark family names;
- hard-coding expected identities such as associativity/group/automaton/Newton names;
- branching on generator IDs;
- using the same concrete symbols at train and transfer time;
- recovering an invariant only after receiving the intended theorem in metadata;
- generating a model that matches training observations but violates hidden interventions;
- declaring two laws equal because their pretty-printed text matches;
- declaring a realization correct because it was generated by the same component that proposes it.

## 3. Common opaque interface

Every domain family must expose only a generic interaction surface such as:

```text
carrier/sort identities
opaque values
available observation operations
available intervention operations
observed transitions/relations
resource/effect boundaries when applicable
experiment budget
```

Family-specific semantic names are stripped or randomized.

The acquisition core sees canonical structure and interaction results, not labels such as `group`, `DFA`, `gravity`, `mutex`, `ring` or `bisimulation`.

## 4. Required outputs

For each held-out world, the system should be capable of producing some subset of the following, with explicit UNKNOWN/NOT_APPLICABLE when unsupported:

```text
constraint claims
candidate equivalence relation(s)
causal structure claim where applicable
invariant claims
validity boundary
falsifier/counterexample profile
candidate StructuralLawClaim
prediction/intervention policy derived from the claim
transfer/equivalence claim to a recoded world
compatible generated model or realization
Residual describing unresolved structure
```

## 5. Family A — finite opaque algebra

Generate finite carriers with one or more opaque operation tables.

No algebraic family name is supplied.

Hidden properties may include combinations of:

```text
closure
identity-like elements
inverse-like relations
associativity
commutativity
idempotence
absorbing elements
distributivity across two operations
homomorphic/isomorphic recodings
```

The point is not to reward naming these properties. The point is to recover structural constraints/invariants that support held-out prediction and transfer.

### Transfer

Generate a randomly relabeled isomorphic copy and, separately, a near-isomorphic negative with one table entry perturbed.

Success requires distinguishing them by structural evidence rather than labels.

## 6. Family B — finite transition systems / automata

Generate opaque finite state-transition systems with hidden observation partitions.

Tasks include:

```text
recover observational equivalence candidates
identify invariant/reachable regions
predict accepted/terminal observations
transfer under state renaming
construct a behaviorally compatible reduced model
find counterexamples to an incorrect quotient
```

The core must not branch on automata terminology.

## 7. Family C — cellular/discrete dynamical worlds

Generate local deterministic update rules over finite grids or graphs.

Hide the concrete rule encoding.

Tasks include:

```text
infer local dependency structure
identify conserved/periodic quantities where present
distinguish transient from regime-stable behavior
predict held-out evolution
transfer under coordinate/symbol recoding
identify boundary conditions where the inferred rule fails
```

Negative worlds deliberately change the update law outside the training region.

## 8. Family D — geometric/incidence structures

Generate finite point/line/adjacency/incidence systems using opaque object IDs.

Tasks include recovering representation-independent relations and invariants under permutations or coordinate changes.

The benchmark must include non-isomorphic structures with matching superficial counts to prevent histogram shortcuts.

## 9. Family E — causal intervention worlds

Generate finite structural causal systems with hidden graph/functional structure.

Expose observational and interventional queries under strict budget.

Tasks include:

```text
separate correlation from intervention-sensitive structure
recover identifiable causal constraints
represent Markov/interventional equivalence when exact orientation is impossible
produce counterfactual predictions within evidenced scope
preserve UNKNOWN where underidentified
```

The system is penalized for inventing a unique causal graph when evidence supports only an equivalence class.

## 10. Family F — stochastic finite processes

Generate finite stochastic transition systems with exact rational probabilities where possible.

Tasks include:

```text
infer probabilistic constraints
recover stationary/invariant quantities when identifiable
calibrate confidence/evidence boundaries
distinguish deterministic from probabilistic claims
avoid upgrading empirical estimates to exact laws
```

This family tests whether the architecture can preserve approximation/statistical semantics without collapsing them into exact truth.

## 11. Family G — distributed protocols

Generate small process/message/state protocols under adversarial schedules.

Tasks include:

```text
recover safety invariants
recover liveness obligations where boundedly testable
identify schedule equivalences
construct violating schedules for false invariants
transfer under process/message renaming
synthesize an admitted implementation/realization under a machine/network profile
```

This family connects law discovery directly to TEV causal/effect and realization semantics.

## 12. Family H — generated program semantics

Generate tiny opaque interpreters/transition semantics over finite programs/states.

Do not expose conventional opcode names.

Tasks include:

```text
infer observational equivalence of programs
recover invariant state relations
identify safe rewrites
construct a semantically equivalent realization under a synthetic MachineField
falsify invalid optimizations
```

This is the most direct test that the same regime architecture applies to compilation.

## 13. Family I — procedurally generated mixed worlds

Combine carriers, transitions, resources, observations and interventions according to random schemas not seen during development.

Some worlds intentionally lack useful nontrivial invariants.

A strong system must be allowed to return:

```text
UNKNOWN
NO_NONTRIVIAL_INVARIANT_FOUND
UNDERIDENTIFIED
RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED
```

rather than hallucinating a law.

## 14. Discovery axis

For each world, measure separately:

```text
D1 constraint recovery
D2 equivalence recovery
D3 invariant recovery
D4 causal recovery where applicable
D5 validity-boundary recovery
D6 falsifier/counterexample quality
D7 held-out prediction
D8 intervention selection/information gain
```

A single aggregate score must not hide systematic failure on causality or boundary detection.

## 15. Transfer axis

Use at least four transfer transformations:

```text
T1 symbol permutation
T2 representation change with same abstract structure
T3 isomorphic copy plus irrelevant structure
T4 near-equivalent negative with one semantically relevant perturbation
```

The system should preserve equivalence claims across T1–T3 when justified and reject T4.

## 16. Realization axis

Given a candidate StructuralLawClaim/Transformation and one or more synthetic MachineFields:

```text
R1 construct at least one candidate realization
R2 reject machines missing required semantic capabilities
R3 preserve regime constraints/invariants
R4 distinguish exact/refinement/approximate relations
R5 produce evidence-bound admission receipt
R6 choose Pareto-admissible candidate under multi-resource cost
R7 generate post-execution observation and update evidence
```

## 17. Generative axis

Given a law claim, ask for a compatible finite model/world not seen in discovery.

Then independently verify:

```text
constraint satisfaction
invariant preservation
causal/equivalence properties where applicable
boundary assumptions
```

Generate both positive and adversarial negative candidates.

## 18. Round-trip axis

For sufficiently identifying worlds:

```text
Law L
 -> admitted realization/model R
 -> generated observations/interventions H
 -> rediscovery L'
 -> governed LawEquivalenceClaim(L,L')
```

Measure whether the same structural class is recovered without requiring identical syntax.

For underidentified worlds, the correct result may be an equivalence class or PROOF_REQUIRED rather than exact recovery.

## 19. Boundary-of-validity tests

Every family must include worlds with regime shifts.

Examples:

```text
operation table changes after hidden context switch
transition rule differs beyond a region
causal mechanism changes under one intervention
distributed protocol assumption fails under packet loss
numeric realization violates an approximation bound outside input range
```

Success requires representing the discovered law as conditional on a boundary/regime rather than universalizing it.

## 20. Counterfactual/negative tests

For every positive invariant, synthesize or search for:

```text
nearby violations
countermodels
adversarial schedules
out-of-regime observations
representation perturbations
```

The system must expose which claim breaks and produce a Residual/counterexample witness.

## 21. Anti-leakage rules

The acquisition core is scanned for:

```text
benchmark family names
generator identifiers
known theorem/law names
hard-coded expected cardinalities
special-case symbol strings
expected invariant literals
```

Generated test seeds and hidden family parameters are inaccessible to the acquisition core.

## 22. Train/development/held-out separation

Use three levels:

```text
DEV
  visible generators and examples for debugging generic infrastructure

HELD-OUT INSTANCE
  unseen parameters/seeds inside known family shape

HELD-OUT FAMILY
  generator family withheld from architecture tuning
```

The strongest claim requires held-out-family transfer.

## 23. Metamorphic tests

A law/discovery pipeline must satisfy metamorphic relations such as:

```text
renaming opaque symbols does not change structural verdict
permuting observation order does not change order-independent claims
adding irrelevant disconnected structure does not change scoped law
removing identifying interventions weakens certainty, never strengthens it
adding a valid counterexample cannot increase support for a universal claim
```

## 24. Evidence calibration

Every output claim reports evidence method/scope.

Metrics distinguish:

```text
proved/exhaustive
translation-validated
finite differential-tested
statistically supported
empirical only
assumed
unknown
```

A system is penalized for epistemic escalation even when the final numerical prediction happens to be correct.

## 25. Resource accounting

Record:

```text
interaction count
search states explored
wall-clock observation (non-semantic measurement)
peak memory observation
proof/verification work
candidate count
counterexample count
```

Resource observations are not part of law semantic identity.

## 26. Capability-vector understanding report

Report independent capabilities:

```text

U_invariant
U_counterfactual

U_transfer

```

At minimum classify:

```text

```

## 28. Strong falsification outcomes

The common architecture should be reconsidered if held-out-family experiments repeatedly show:

```text
no reusable representation of equivalence/invariant across families;
useful laws require adding family-specific primitive operations to the core;
quotienting systematically destroys information needed for intervention;
model generation cannot reproduce held-out consequences of discovered claims;
realization preservation requires a fundamentally unrelated architecture;
structural transfer fails despite successful within-instance prediction.
```

## 29. Positive evidence threshold

Even broad success does not establish a literal “Universal Natural Law”.

The permitted claim would be narrower:

> Across the tested families, a provider-neutral restriction/equivalence/invariant architecture supports discovery, transfer and realization without family-specific semantic branches in the core.

Stronger scientific claims require stronger domains, physical evidence and formal results.
